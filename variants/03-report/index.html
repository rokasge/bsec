Scope of audit services

Five service lines. They are usually bought in combination: most engagements start with the application or the platform, and pull in research or disclosure coordination when a finding warrants it.

  1. Web Application & API Penetration Testing

    Core service

    Manual testing to find exploitable flaws before attackers do. Authentication, access control, injection, business-logic abuse and API authorization are all in scope.

    Typical targets
    customer-facing web applications, internal tools, REST and GraphQL APIs, partner integrations
    You receive
    findings with reproduction steps and a remediation per item

  2. Vulnerability Research (Auth Bypass, Injection, Deserialization)

    Deep dive

    Deep-dive research into authentication, injection and unsafe deserialization in custom and enterprise software — the classes of flaw that survive automated scanning and surface later as an incident.

    Typical targets
    custom authentication flows, serialization boundaries, ORM and query layers, trust boundaries between services
    You receive
    a documented path from entry point to impact, with the conditions required to reach it

  3. Enterprise Software & CMS Security Reviews

    Review

    Security review of enterprise platforms and CMS deployments, from configuration through to source-level issues — covering the layer you own rather than only the vendor’s.

    Typical targets
    enterprise platform deployments, CMS installations, their plugins and extensions, hosting and permission configuration
    You receive
    configuration and source-level findings ranked by what an attacker reaches first

  4. Android Application Assessments

    Review

    Mobile application assessment covering storage, transport, authentication and inter-process communication.

    Typical targets
    released and pre-release Android builds, their on-device storage, network transport, session handling and IPC surface
    You receive
    findings mapped to the component that carries them, with a fix per component

  5. CVE Coordination & Advisory

    Disclosure

    Coordinating disclosure with vendors and assigning CVEs where warranted. Useful whether the affected software is yours or a third-party component you ship.

    Typical targets
    findings in third-party and enterprise software, and in your own products where a public advisory is the right outcome
    You receive
    vendor liaison, coordinated timelines and advisory text prepared on your behalf

How an engagement runs — methodology

Four stages, in this order. The first three are what you are buying; the fourth is the part most providers charge for again.

  1. Scopingbefore any testing
  2. Testingagainst agreed scope
  3. Reportingone written report
  4. Retestfree, no time limit

  1. Scoping

    We agree targets, rules of engagement and out-of-scope systems in writing before any testing begins. You know exactly what will be touched, by whom, and when — and so does your infrastructure team.

    Output A signed scope and rules-of-engagement document.

  2. Testing

    Manual assessment against the agreed scope, reported as it progresses so findings are never a surprise at the end. If something urgent turns up on day one, you hear about it on day one.

    Output Interim notes on high and critical findings while testing is still running.

  3. Reporting

    A written report. Each finding carries severity, reproduction steps, business impact and a concrete remediation — the format shown in section 2 of this page. Written for the people who have to fix it and the people who have to sign off on the risk.

    Output The report, with a summary table and one full entry per finding.

  4. Retest

    After remediation, findings are re-tested and the report is updated. Free of charge and without a time limit. Fix it next sprint or next year — the retest still stands.

    Output Updated findings with their remediation status confirmed.

Working terms & disclosure policy

Five clauses, applied to every engagement without negotiation.

  1. Encrypted communications accepted

    PGP on request. Share a key before scoping and everything from the first email onward is encrypted.

  2. Zero exploit code published prior to a fix

    Nothing technical goes public while a fix is pending, regardless of severity.

  3. Zero client names published without written permission

    No logos, no case studies, no “a company in the fintech sector” hints. Written permission or silence.

  4. Medium-severity issues: courtesy credit / hall of fame

    If a finding is disclosed publicly at all, credit is offered rather than assumed.

  5. Critical-severity: full write-up + CVE coordination

    Where the impact warrants it, the finding gets a full write-up and is coordinated with the vendor through CVE assignment.
