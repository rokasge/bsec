The engagement timeline

Four stages. Read top to bottom and you know exactly what you are buying, what you receive, and when.

stage

Scoping

Before any testing begins

Agree targets, rules of engagement, and out-of-scope systems in writing before any testing begins. Nothing is touched until you have signed off on the boundary.

What you supply

  • In-scope hosts, applications, API endpoints, and accounts
  • Named emergency contact and any change-freeze windows
  • Out-of-scope systems, stated explicitly rather than assumed

What you receive

  • A written rules-of-engagement document both sides sign
  • A schedule: testing window, reporting date, retest date
  • A single point of contact for the whole engagement

How audits start here

Most engagements begin with a short call and an email thread. Send the shape of what you need — targets, timeline, compliance driver — and scoping is quoted from that.

stage

Testing

Manual, against the agreed scope

Manual assessment against the agreed scope, reported as it progresses so findings are never a surprise at the end. Anything urgent reaches you the day it is confirmed.

Testing performed during this stage

  • Web Application & API Penetration Testing

    Manual testing to find exploitable flaws before attackers do. Authentication, access control, injection, business-logic abuse, and API authorization.

  • Vulnerability Research (Auth Bypass, Injection, Deserialization)

    Deep-dive research into authentication, injection, and unsafe deserialization in custom and enterprise software.

  • Enterprise Software & CMS Security Reviews

    Security review of enterprise platforms and CMS deployments, from configuration through to source-level issues.

  • Android Application Assessments

    Mobile application assessment covering storage, transport, authentication, and inter-process communication.

  • CVE Coordination & Advisory

    Coordinating disclosure with vendors and assigning CVEs where warranted.

Working terms during testing

  • Encrypted communications accepted (PGP on request)
  • Zero exploit code published prior to a fix
  • Zero client names published without written permission

stage

Reporting

A written report you can act on

A written report where each finding carries severity, reproduction steps, business impact, and a concrete remediation — written for the engineer who has to fix it and the manager who has to fund it.

Every finding includes

  • Severity, rated against business impact rather than raw CVSS alone
  • Reproduction steps detailed enough for your own team to verify
  • The business impact of exploitation, stated plainly
  • A concrete remediation, not a generic hardening checklist

Reporting guarantees

  • Findings are reported as testing progresses, not held back
  • Critical issues are communicated immediately on confirmation
  • Findings are written to be handed to a developer unedited

stage

Retest

After remediation · free, no time limit

After remediation, findings are re-tested and the report is updated. Free of charge and without a time limit — you bring the fix, the retest happens.

What the updated report gives you

  • A clear fixed / not-fixed status against every original finding
  • An updated document you can attach to a compliance submission
  • A statement of residual risk where a fix only mitigates

Publication & disclosure terms

These terms apply to everything found during the engagement, from the first confirmed finding through to publication after a fix.

  • Encrypted communications accepted (PGP on request)
  • Zero exploit code published prior to a fix
  • Zero client names published without written permission
  • Medium-severity issues: courtesy credit / hall of fame
  • Critical-severity: full write-up + CVE coordination
